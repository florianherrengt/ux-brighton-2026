# UX Brighton research reading list

Talk: 6 November 2026. Copies saved: 8 October 2026.

This folder contains the 26 works in the supplied reading list. The files in `files/` are original PDFs where available, PDF snapshots of public web pages, or (for the Google guidebook) the publisher's ZIP of chapters and worksheets. Each entry below links to the local copy and the live source. The live source remains the place to check for updates and to cite in the talk.

## Read these six first

1. [David — *The Dynamo and the Computer*](files/01-david-dynamo-computer.pdf): why electricity's productivity gains required changes to factories and work.
2. [Devine — *From Shafts to Wires*](files/02-devine-shafts-wires.pdf): the physical move from central shafts and belts to electric motors.
3. [Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson — *The Productivity J-Curve*](files/03-productivity-j-curve.pdf): why complementary investment can delay measured gains.
4. [Stanford — *Canaries in the Coal Mine?*](files/09-canaries-employment.pdf): descriptive evidence on employment and hiring among young workers in AI-exposed occupations.
5. [Figma — *2026 AI Report*](files/07-figma-2026-ai-report.pdf): survey evidence of design and development roles crossing over.
6. [Anthropic — *Demystifying Evals for AI Agents*](files/23-demystifying-agent-evals.pdf): a practical route from defining success to testing agent behaviour.

The papers and surveys support a claim that tasks, tools, and hiring patterns are changing. They do **not** establish that AI will eliminate UX design as a profession. A prediction about the traditional UX role shrinking remains an argument to make from the evidence, not a measured result of these sources.

## 1. Historical technological revolutions

**Main idea:** New technology can change an industry's whole system of work. Its largest benefits may require new processes and organisations.

1. **The Dynamo and the Computer: An Historical Perspective on the Modern Productivity Paradox** — Paul A. David, 1990. Compares the lag between electrification and productivity growth with the early computer era; central to the factory redesign story. [Local PDF](files/01-david-dynamo-computer.pdf) · [Journal record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2006600) · [PDF copy source](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/automation/1990-david.pdf). The local seven-page copy is a mirror of the 1990 article.

2. **From Shafts to Wires: Historical Perspective on Electrification** — Warren D. Devine Jr., 1983. Traces the move from steam-powered shaft and belt systems to group and individual electric drives, with effects on energy use and productivity. [Local PDF](files/02-devine-shafts-wires.pdf) · [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/from-shafts-to-wires-historical-perspective-on-electrification/500078D9B4764BA1109A7967437CF226) · [PDF copy source](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/automation/1983-devine.pdf). Cambridge currently shows an abstract and an access request; the local full-text copy comes from a third-party mirror. The supplied list's “full PDF” description does not apply to the Cambridge page.

3. **The Productivity J-Curve: How Intangibles Complement General Purpose Technologies** — Erik Brynjolfsson, Daniel Rock and Chad Syverson, 2021. Explains how investment in skills, processes and other intangibles can precede measured productivity gains from a general-purpose technology. [Local PDF](files/03-productivity-j-curve.pdf) · [Published article](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/mac.20180386) · [PDF copy source](https://ide.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/jcurve.pdf). **Version note:** the local full text is the authors' 2018 NBER working-paper version, not the final 2021 journal layout.

4. **Reshaping the Landscape: The Impact and Diffusion of the Tractor in American Agriculture, 1910–1960** — Alan L. Olmstead and Paul W. Rhode, 2001. Examines how horses and tractors coexisted and how adoption depended on farm methods and capital replacement; it argues that the shift was faster than commonly assumed once those factors are counted. [Local PDF](files/04-tractor-diffusion.pdf) · [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7947) · [Author-hosted PDF](https://faculty.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/alolmstead/Recent_Publications/Reshaping_the_Landscape.pdf).

## 2. What AI is already changing

**Main idea:** Observed AI use can show which work is being assisted or automated today; it does not by itself predict whole-job replacement.

5. **Introducing the Anthropic Economic Index** — Anthropic, 2025. Analyses real Claude use across occupations and distinguishes assistance from automation. [Local page snapshot](files/05-anthropic-economic-index.pdf) · [Original article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/the-anthropic-economic-index).

6. **Anthropic Economic Index: AI's Impact on Software Development** — Anthropic, 2025. Examines 500,000 Claude.ai and Claude Code interactions, including substantial web and UI development use. The reported 79% automation classification applies specifically to Claude Code conversations, not all conversations in the study. [Local page snapshot](files/06-anthropic-software-development.pdf) · [Original article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/impact-software-development).

7. **Figma's 2026 AI Report: Can AI Help Us Collaborate Better?** — Figma, 2026. Figma's survey reports designers participating in development rising from 21% to 41%, and developers participating in design from 44% to 60%. [Local page snapshot](files/07-figma-2026-ai-report.pdf) · [Original article](https://www.figma.com/blog/2026-ai-report/). This is company survey evidence, not independent labour-market data.

8. **Why Demand for Designers Is on the Rise** — Figma, 2026. A counterpoint based on Figma's survey of design managers: some employers report continued or increased demand for designers, especially senior ones. [Local page snapshot](files/08-figma-design-demand.pdf) · [Original article](https://www.figma.com/blog/why-demand-for-designers-is-on-the-rise/). Treat it as a survey of respondents, not a measure of all design hiring.

## 3. Jobs, displacement and junior hiring

**Main idea:** Hiring patterns are changing. Some patterns are consistent with AI-related displacement, but these sources do not prove that AI caused every decline.

9. **Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence** — Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar and Ruyu Chen, revised August 2026. Reports employment for workers aged 22–25 in highly exposed occupations 19% below the trend for less-exposed peers. The authors describe this as evidence of a pattern, not a causal estimate. [Local revised PDF](files/09-canaries-employment.pdf) · [Stanford publication](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/).

10. **Labor Market Impacts of AI: A New Measure and Early Evidence** — Maxim Massenkoff and Peter McCrory, Anthropic, March 2026. Combines model capability with observed AI use; finds tentative evidence of slower hiring for young workers in exposed jobs but no systematic increase in unemployment. [Local page snapshot](files/10-anthropic-labor-market.pdf) · [Original report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts).

11. **A Snapshot of Entry-Level Hiring in the UK** — UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology and LinkedIn, June 2026. Reports annual falls of 27% in entry-level software-engineering hiring and 28% in graphic-design hiring while placing them in a broader weak market. The report does not establish AI as the cause. [Local page snapshot](files/11-uk-entry-level-hiring.pdf) · [Original report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/entry-level-hiring-in-the-uk-a-snapshot/a-snapshot-of-entry-level-hiring-in-the-uk).

12. **The Labor Market Is Tilting Toward Seniority** — Felix Aidala and Sneha Puri, Indeed Hiring Lab, July 2026. Tracks rising senior postings alongside falling entry and mid-level postings; discusses several possible explanations. [Local page snapshot](files/12-indeed-seniority.pdf) · [Original article](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/the-labor-market-is-tilting-toward-seniority/).

13. **The SignalFire State of Tech Talent Report — 2025** — SignalFire, 2025. Finds new-graduate hiring at big technology firms and startups substantially below 2019 levels; the report discusses multiple drivers. [Local page snapshot](files/13-signalfire-tech-talent.pdf) · [Original report](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025). The figures come from SignalFire's own data and analysis.

14. **Record Graduate Job Applications** — Institute of Student Employers. Reports an average of 140 applications per graduate vacancy, and 205 for digital and IT roles, in its surveyed period. [Local public-article snapshot](files/14-ise-graduate-applications.pdf) · [Original article](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/410/record_graduate_job_applications). The underlying Student Recruitment Survey is a separate member/paid report and is not included.

15. **Design Economy 2022** — Design Council, 2022. Provides pre-generative-AI context, including 76% employment growth in UK digital design from 2011 to 2020. [Local publisher PDF](files/15-design-economy-2022.pdf) · [Original PDF](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/fileadmin/uploads/dc/Documents/Design_Economy_2022_Full_Report.pdf).

## 4. UX case studies: solve the problem, not the interface

**Main idea:** Good design can remove work, change a process, or make an existing capability easier to use.

16. **Redesigning Microsoft Office** — Jensen Harris, first-hand account. Describes the research and principles behind Office 2007's redesign, including the finding that 90% of users used less than 10% of its features. [Local page snapshot](files/16-microsoft-office-redesign.pdf) · [Original account](https://jensenharris.com/home/office). The original page says “Redesigning,” where the supplied list said “Designing.”

17. **The $300 Million Button** — Jared M. Spool, 2009. Describes replacing forced account registration with a Continue button and reports a $300 million annual revenue increase. [Local page snapshot](files/17-300-million-button.pdf) · [Original account](https://articles.centercentre.com/three_hund_million_button/). The dollar amount is a reported client result, not an independently audited estimate.

18. **The PalmPilot** — Computer History Museum. Covers Jeff Hawkins's wooden prototype and the decision to make the handheld a companion to the PC. [Local page snapshot](files/18-palmpilot.pdf) · [Original exhibit](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/story/321).

19. **Action 2 Case Study: Redesigning Services** — GOV.UK, 2014. Describes a Lasting Power of Attorney prototype that asked users for 70% less information. [Local page snapshot](files/19-redesigning-services.pdf) · [Original case study](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/case-study-on-action-2-redesigning-services/action-2-case-study-redesigning-services--2). The page describes a prototype still in testing, not a proven later service outcome.

20. **On YouTube's Recommendation System** — Cristos Goodrow, YouTube, 2021. Explains the move from click signals to watch time and then valued watch time, illustrating the importance of choosing the right outcome measure. [Local page snapshot](files/20-youtube-recommendations.pdf) · [Original article](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/).

21. **Service Design: Isn't It Just UX with a Different Name?** — Sūryanāga Poyzer, UK Government Digital Service, 2016. Argues for designing the whole service, including processes behind the visible interface. [Local page snapshot](files/21-service-design-ux.pdf) · [Original article](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2016/10/04/service-design-isnt-it-just-ux-with-a-different-name/).

## 5. Agentic UX, evaluation and practical adaptation

**Main idea:** When systems can act, designers also need to define permissions, limits, success criteria, and routes to recover from errors.

22. **Building Effective Agents** — Anthropic, December 2024. Introduces agent workflows, tools, and the distinction between fixed workflows and more autonomous agents. [Local page snapshot](files/22-building-effective-agents.pdf) · [Original article](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents). Anthropic now notes on the page that much of its tooling landscape has changed since publication.

23. **Demystifying Evals for AI Agents** — Anthropic, January 2026. Explains how to specify success, build test tasks and graders, and use evaluation to detect agent failures. [Local page snapshot](files/23-demystifying-agent-evals.pdf) · [Original article](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents).

24. **Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction** — Saleema Amershi and twelve coauthors, CHI 2019. Proposes and evaluates 18 guidelines covering expectations, feedback, correction, control, and recovery. [Local PDF](files/24-human-ai-guidelines.pdf) · [Publisher record](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/) · [Academic PDF copy](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs194h/2023/wi/readings/restricted/Saleema_AI_UX_Guidelines.pdf). The Microsoft-hosted PDF denied direct download here; the local file is the same paper from a Stanford course reading page.

25. **People + AI Guidebook** — Google PAIR. Practical chapters and worksheets for designing AI products around user needs, trust, control, failure, and evaluation. [Local publisher ZIP](files/25-people-ai-guidebook.zip) · [Current guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/) · [Publisher ZIP](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/legacy-guidebook-assets/assets/People%20%2B%20AI%20Guidebook%20-%20All%20Chapters%20and%20Worksheets.zip). The ZIP contains six chapter PDFs and six worksheets; the live site may have newer material.

26. **Identifying a Good Service** — Louise Downe, UK Government Digital Service, 2016. Defines a service around a need the user recognises and the whole task they must complete. [Local page snapshot](files/26-identifying-good-service.pdf) · [Original article](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2016/04/22/identifying-a-good-service/).

## Copy and access notes

- Page snapshots were printed from the public pages on 8 October 2026. They preserve the readable page as it appeared then, but navigation, embedded media and interactive features are not included. Some Anthropic fonts look correct on the page but do not extract cleanly as searchable PDF text.
- The David and Devine PDFs came from third-party mirrors; the J-Curve PDF is an earlier author-hosted working paper; the Human-AI Guidelines PDF came from an academic course host. These origins and version differences are noted beside the entries above.
