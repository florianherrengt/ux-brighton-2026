# What happens when AI can do your job?

I don't know if you've read the news recently, but apparently we are going to be out of jobs.

We're having CEOs from major companies saying that they're firing everyone to replace them with AI. The media is describing it as a bloodbath or the AI job apocalypse. It's going to be really bad.

Why can they do all of this? Because AI is getting really good. And it's true. It is getting very good. The models are getting better really fast. On several benchmarks, they're better or getting close to being better than humans.

The replacement of horses by cars in the United States is uncannily similar. Cars are getting better slowly and slowly. Then they start to get really good and there's a total collapse of horses in the US.

Why would you buy a horse when you can buy a car?

So. obviously, we are the horses and the cars are the AI. It's a good story. It's also irrelevant.

## You are not a horse

You can learn, you can adapt, you can do new things.

But horses, they can't. The only thing they could do was convert food into mechanical work.

A horse couldn't just go and say, “Hey, you know what? Let me just retrain as a mechanic.” It can't change what it's doing. Humans can.

So the next natural step is to just use AI, right?

## AI won't take your job. Someone with AI will.

Bullshit. They want us to imagine a world where we still have accountants, lawyers, designers and developers doing exactly the same job.

They'll just keep doing the same thing, except they'll have AI helping them do their job better, faster, stronger.

But that's not what we're scared of. That's not why the industry has invested trillions of dollars into this new technology.

We're scared of complete replacement. AI doing the job of the people instead of people doing the job with AI.

## Nobody knows the future

So if we don't know the future, why am I here talking to you right now? Well, we can look at the past.

We can look at previous technological changes that happened and what people have done and what they ended up getting wrong or right.

But before we even do that, let's have a look at some of the headlines. Headlines from the past. Quotes from people who were very respected, very well known in their fields. They knew what they were talking about and somehow still go it wrong.

In 2016, Geoffrey Hinton said: “It's just completely obvious that in five years deep learning is going to do better than radiologists.”

And he even went further by saying that if you were a radiologist back then, you were like the cartoon coyote where you've just walked off the cliff and there's just no ground under you. You just haven't realised yet.

Imagine being a 25 - 30 years old radiologist reading this back then. Imagine how scary that would have been to read these headlines.
Afterall, you don't know anything about deep learning but the people who do think it can do the job.

VCs saying that AI would displace 50% of jobs by 2027. The CEO of Anthropic keeps saying that all the developers are going to be replaced. So he said earlier this year that the models will be able to do everything that software engineers can do end to end.

So I guess they still have a few months to deliver on their promise. But so far, it's not happening.

They keep making extraordinary claims with no extraordinary evidence to back them up.

This goes back all the way to 1960. Herbert Simon (Nobel Prize for economics) said that machines would be capable of doing anything a man can do in twenty years.
In 2011, Foxconn said they would replace workers with one million robots in three years. They barely managed to do 50,000.
Elon Musk said that there would be no humans in production. But what they love even more than replacing the humans is to scare them.

This idea of replacing humans with machines isn't new.

OpenAI said that GPT-2 was too dangerous to be released.

That was in 2017 and 2019. Just put that into perspective. Think about what the tech looked like back then compared to what we have today.

We have open-weight model more powerful than we could even imagine 10 years ago running on our phones.

I'm not saying this to mock those people or point out how wrong they were. I just want you to remember that **nobody** knows the future.

So, we can ignore the noise for a while and relax. Take a step back. Start thinking about what we can do.

## Replacing the steam engine

This was a typical steam factory. A big steam engine that powers the whole factory, transferring the power through the shafts and belts to the machines. Great system.

But then electricity arrived. It was a game changer. Can you imagine if you didn't have electricity today? So what did they do?

They just replaced the steam engine with an electric one. They just put an electric motor in place of the steam one. It was a little bit better, faster, stronger. But it wasn't game changing.

This is what we're doing today. We're looking at what we have and think “Let's add some AI to it. Let's sprinkle some here and there.”.

But the real breakthrough came only when we completely redesigned factories around electricity.

No more shafts. No more belts.

It's a completely different factory. A completely different system.

We need to redesign the way we're working.

## The man with the red flag

There used to be a time in England when, if you had a steam road locomotive, you'd have to have a man walking in front of it holding a red flag.

The machine was too fast. Too dangerous. So what did they do? They just slowed it down to the speed of a walking man.

Eventually we fully redesigned our cities, for better or worse, around cars. We don't need a man with a red flag in front of every car anymore.

If your job is to watch AI produce the output and then at the end you just verify that it hasn't done anything crazy, you're a person holding a red flag.

## William Durant

Let's have a look at a success story.

William Durant was the biggest horse-carriage producer in the US when the automobile arrives. He didn't like it. It was threatening his business.

Eventually he managed to get himself convinced that it could be a good thing. He tried it, took over Buick and later founded General Motors.

Why is this an interesting story? Because Durant realised that he wasn't in the business of selling horse carriages.

He was selling individual transportation.

When the new technology arrived, he just kept doing the same thing, but with different tech. Just sell cars instead of horse carriages.

So what is it that you do? Are you in the business of drawing little boxes on the screen and choosing the colours? Are you typing code really fast? Or are you building great software?

There's new tech. You can use it to build even better software.

Just like William Durant back then, don't be scared. Try it. Learn and make the best use of it.

## George Stephenson

But maybe Durant was already successful man. He was weatlhy from his previous business. He already had the skills. He knew how to run a business and he could afford to experiment.

But George Stephenson was a nobody. He just worked in a coal mine. He wasn't doing anything special.

At some point he's put in charge of operating a steam engine. But simply operating the steam engine is not enough for him.

He wants to understand it. He wants to understand how it works.

So he just pulls it apart, looks at all the pieces, understands how they connect to each other, how this whole thing works and he puts it back. Then he does it again and again until one day he just knows everything about it.

When there is a problem, he immediately knows how to fix it. He also has ideas about how to improve it.

Don't just type things into Claude or Codex and press the buttons. Go and learn.

What is a token? What is a context window? What is quantisation?

## How to use AI badly

That's enough about the past. Let's talk about today.

I do not know what the future looks like. I do not know what the new factory is going to look like.

However, I do know that there are bad ways to use AI. All of these things will diminish the value of your work.

- Do the same work faster: we need less of you.
- Make your job easier: we can hire someone cheaper.
- Produce more unchecked output: that's just slop.
- Avoid learning: if you just say, “I don't know how to do this. Let me just ask Claude and it'll do it for me.” then what's your added value here?

What are you proposing if you're asking AI to do something and you don't know how to do it? What stops me from just asking it directly?

## The meat proxy

That brings us to the last, worst AI user: the meat proxy.

> A human who relays work to and from an AI without adding meaningful judgment or value.

I'm sure everyone has had someone like this in their team at some point. You ask a question to someone by email or Slack and they reply to you with a wall of text that is obviously AI-generated.

Why would you message that person again? What's the point?

Just go and ask Claude or ChatGPT directly. You don't need them anymore.

If I curate a ticket, a description, what to do and then I give it to you and all you do is say, “Read this ticket and do the work.”

What are you adding? Why are you even here? Can't I just ask Claude to do it directly?

## Why were you hired in the first place?

You need to start thinking about why you were hired in the first place.

Why you? Seriously, why were you hired in the first place? Why should I hire you?

Anyone in the world, a laptop, an internet connection: they can do your job, right?

I went and pulled about 1,500 UX Designer jobs, and this is pretty much what you would expect from a UX designer. Collaboration and communication. They want you to know Figma. They want you to do some user testing and we can see they're starting to ask for AI.

But these skills are not particularly hard to find.

This is an eight billion people planet and anyone with a laptop and an internet connection is a potential employee. So why you?

You live in London, Paris, San Francisco, New York. All of these cities are incredibly expensive. I have to pay you a lot of money.

It is because the cost of getting it wrong is incredibly high.

The cost of hiring the wrong person is much higher than any of the other costs. The salary of the person is nothing compared to hiring the wrong person.

I'm sure you've worked with people before where you were thinking, “If this person wasn't here, things would be easier.”
There are people out there who are active liabilities. They contribute to the team in a negative way because they make terrible decisions.

Well, good news, because now, thanks to AI, they can do it 10x faster. By the time you've had your lunch, they can completely destroy everything.

## You are paid to make good decisions

But not only to make good decisions. To make good decisions with incomplete information, under constraints and without constant supervision.

The last part is the most important one: without constant supervision.

If we're paid to make good decisions, attention is all we have. How much brain power do you have in a day? This is the only thing that is actually useful to our job.

So if we want to do more, if we want to produce more, we only have a few options:

1. Reduce the quality. We don't want to do this.
1. Delegate to the AI. Obviously, we're not doing that either.
1. Automating things

## We haven't redesigned the factory yet

We're still in the same old-fashioned way of doing things.

I don't know the future but this is my bet. This is what I think will happen and someone can quote me in ten years later to show how wrong I was.

But I believe that we need to start automating entire processes.

We need systems that will decide, execute and verify without constant supervision.

## Never correct the AI

Next time you use Claude, Codex, any of the tools that you're using, just never correct it.

Make it a rule for yourself that you will never correct the AI.

Every time it does something that you don't like, you just go back and you start thinking: what can I do so this never happens again? What can I do to stop it from doing stupid things like this?

If you keep doing it, you will slowly start approaching 100% accuracy without supervision.

Keep improving it until you can trust it that it won't make that mistake again. And if you can't do that, you failed. Go back to the beginning or stop using AI for this.
